Menu bar utility for macOS 13+

Bluetooth that knows when to quiet down.

BlueNap switches your Mac's Bluetooth off the instant it falls asleep, and switches it back on the instant it wakes — so the headphones paired to your phone stay yours all night.

The problem

The 3am handoff nobody asked for

Pair the same headphones or speaker with your phone and your Mac, and a sleeping Mac becomes a rival. It can wake just enough to grab the Bluetooth connection mid-podcast, cutting your phone's audio for a beat while nothing on the Mac even needed it.

BlueNap listens for the sleep and wake notifications macOS already sends, and reacts to them directly — no polling, no background daemon guessing.

How it works

Three states, one listener

BlueNap runs quietly as a menu bar app and hooks into the same sleep/wake events macOS uses internally, toggling power through the system's own Bluetooth controller.

Awake

Your Mac behaves normally. Bluetooth stays on and every paired accessory works exactly as it always has.

Asleep

The moment your Mac sleeps, BlueNap powers Bluetooth off entirely — or, if you've chosen specific devices, disconnects just those.

Wake

On wake, BlueNap immediately restores whatever state Bluetooth was in before — no delay, no manual toggle.

Selective disconnect

Keep Bluetooth on, lose only what's in the way

Sometimes killing Bluetooth entirely is overkill — you might still want a Bluetooth keyboard or trackpad staying paired. Open Settings → Disconnect on sleep and tick the specific devices that should drop when your Mac sleeps.

  • Select one or more devices and Bluetooth itself stays powered on — only those devices disconnect.
  • Leave nothing selected and BlueNap falls back to switching Bluetooth off completely.
  • Some accessories reconnect on their own initiative, so treat selective disconnect as best-effort with auto-reconnecting hardware.

Menu bar icon, open

Lives in the menu bar

One icon, then it gets out of your way

BlueNap sits in the menu bar as a small SwiftUI-native icon. Click it and a two-line menu drops down — Settings and Quit — nothing else to learn.

  • Untick "Show menu bar icon" in Settings and it disappears immediately.
  • Relaunch the app any time (open -a BlueNap) and the icon returns with Settings open.
  • Optionally enable "Launch at login" so it's always watching from boot.

Install

Up and running in a minute

  1. Get a build

    Download the latest release, or build it yourself from source with make all.

  2. Move it to Applications

    Open BlueNap.app from your Applications folder like any other Mac app.

  3. Turn on Launch at login

    Optional, from the Settings window — so BlueNap is watching from the moment you log in.

  4. Sleep your Mac

    That's it. Bluetooth will power down on sleep and come back the moment you wake it.

zsh — hide the icon
# hide the menu bar icon from Terminal
defaults write io.github.root913.bluenap hideIcon -bool true && killall BlueNap

# bring it back — the icon is the only way to reopen Settings
defaults delete io.github.root913.bluenap hideIcon && killall BlueNap

Worth knowing

  • Not compatible with "Allow your Apple Watch to unlock your Mac" — that feature needs Bluetooth to stay live during sleep.
  • Can't ship on the App Store: BlueNap toggles Bluetooth power through a private macOS API.

FAQ

Good to know

Can I disconnect only some devices on sleep?

Yes. Open Settings and tick the devices you want disconnected when your Mac sleeps. With at least one selected, Bluetooth itself stays on and only those devices drop; with none selected, BlueNap switches Bluetooth off entirely instead.

How do I hide the BlueNap icon?

Untick "Show menu bar icon" in Settings and it's gone right away. You can also do it from Terminal — see the snippet above.

I hid the icon — how do I get Settings back?

The icon is the only way to open Settings, so just relaunch the app with open -a BlueNap. The icon reappears and Settings opens automatically.

Does this work with any Bluetooth accessory?

Most of them. A few devices, especially headphones, try to reconnect to a sleeping Mac on their own initiative, so selective disconnect is best-effort with hardware like that rather than a hard guarantee.
